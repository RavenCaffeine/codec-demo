Abstract

Neural speech codecs are widely used in modern speech and multimodal systems, yet their performance depends on many intertwined design and training variables. Existing codec comparisons often change several variables simultaneously, making it difficult to determine which variables matter most. In this work, we conduct a controlled empirical study using a unified codec backbone and evaluation pipeline. We independently vary five variables: training data scale, temporal resolution, latent feature dimension, codebook width, and RVQ depth. Our results show that temporal resolution has a strong impact on codec quality, while latent dimension can be reduced substantially with limited performance degradation. Increasing RVQ depth is also more effective than simply enlarging the codebook, and training data exhibits clear diminishing returns. These findings provide insights for speech codec design.

Index Terms — neural audio codec, speech tokenization, quality evaluation
