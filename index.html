Abstract

Neural speech codecs are widely used in speech compression, text-to-speech generation, and speech language models. Their performance depends on many design and training choices, while different applications may value reconstruction quality, perceptual quality, linguistic content, pitch, speaker identity, or compression efficiency differently. However, existing codec comparisons often change several factors at the same time, making it difficult to understand the effect of each design choice. In this work, we study how major codec design factors affect different aspects of speech reconstruction under a same backbone and evaluation pipeline. We vary training data scale, latent frame rate, representation dimensionality, codebook size, Residual Vector Quantization (RVQ) depth, and training strategy while keeping other settings fixed whenever possible. We evaluate all configurations with the same metrics for spectral reconstruction, perceptual quality, linguistic content, pitch, speaker similarity, and compression efficiency. All the code, dataset, evaluation pipeline and pretrained model presented in this paper will be open-sourced upon paper acceptance.
